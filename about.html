About Coral Bleaching

Understanding the threat to our oceans and the importance of early detection

What is Coral Bleaching?

Coral bleaching occurs when corals lose their symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) due to environmental stress. This process turns corals white and can lead to widespread death if conditions don't improve.

The primary cause is rising ocean temperatures, but other factors like pollution, sedimentation, and ocean acidification also contribute to this phenomenon.

When corals bleach, they become more susceptible to disease and mortality, severely impacting marine biodiversity and the ecosystems that depend on coral reefs.

Why Early Detection Matters

Early detection of bleaching conditions allows for timely intervention and mitigation strategies. By monitoring environmental factors, we can predict potential bleaching events before they become catastrophic.

Our tool uses machine learning to analyze key environmental parameters and provide risk assessments, helping researchers and conservationists take proactive measures.

Protecting coral reefs is crucial for marine biodiversity, coastal protection, and the millions of people who depend on reef ecosystems for food and livelihoods.
